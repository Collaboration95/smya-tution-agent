SMYA S0-01 - SIMULATED STAKEHOLDER REQUIREMENTS
================================================

Discovery decision
------------------

- Tutor-capacity hypothesis: unvalidated but plausible; retain for the
  prototype.
- Economic buyer and sponsor: small tuition-centre owner/administrator.
- Primary operator: working tutor.
- Downstream users: students and parents/guardians.
- Selected scope: one primary Mathematics class, fractions, using three to
  five sub-skills.
- Highest-value workflow:

  completed student work -> deterministic marking -> evidence-backed weakness
  proposal -> targeted practice draft -> tutor approval -> assignment

- Secondary workflow: parent progress drafting after trustworthy mastery
  history exists.
- Claims that must not be made: validated SME demand, willingness to pay, real
  tutor time savings, or proven return on investment.

What the centre owner/administrator wants
----------------------------------------

- Help each tutor support more students without immediately adding headcount.
- Improve consistency between tutors and classes.
- Give parents credible evidence of progress and next steps.
- Retain control over curriculum, staff permissions, student access, and
  external communications.
- Measure net time saved after tutor review and editing, rather than measuring
  only AI generation speed.
- Protect children's information and prevent accidental or unapproved
  communication.
- Introduce a lightweight workflow rather than replace the centre's operation
  with a large learning-management system.
- Keep costs, latency, and failure behaviour predictable.
- Continue the normal workflow when the model provider is unavailable.

The owner/administrator is likely to reject:

- Large setup or data-migration requirements.
- Per-student AI costs without measurable operational benefit.
- Autonomous parent communication or assessment assignment.
- A process that creates as much tutor-review work as it removes.
- Generic AI output without curriculum grounding, evidence, or auditability.

What the working tutor wants
----------------------------

- See the exact questions, answers, and mistakes behind a proposed diagnosis.
- Avoid permanently labelling a student from one poor attempt.
- Receive a short, usable follow-up activity rather than generic AI prose.
- Accept, edit, reject, or request more evidence quickly.
- Automate routine objective marking while returning ambiguous answers for
  human review.
- Review students in batches or at class level rather than processing a large
  alert queue one student at a time.
- Retain corrections so the same generated-content problem is not repeatedly
  fixed.
- Avoid duplicate data entry and unnecessary workflow switching.
- Remain accountable for teaching, assignment, and communication decisions.

The tutor's key objection is approval burden. If reviewing generated material
takes as long as preparing it manually, the product has failed.

Plausible current workflow
--------------------------

1. The tutor gathers homework, quiz results, and personal observations from
   several places.
2. The tutor manually inspects errors and infers which topics or sub-skills
   need attention.
3. Most students receive a common worksheet; the tutor manually differentiates
   material when time permits.
4. The tutor marks work, explains mistakes, and decides what to teach next.
5. Progress is remembered or recorded informally.
6. Parent updates are assembled later from incomplete notes.

Likely pain points are fragmented evidence, repetitive worksheet preparation,
inconsistent personalisation, delayed feedback, and parent-report catch-up.

Illustrative timing assumptions
-------------------------------

These ranges are working assumptions for a future benchmark:

- Review and diagnose: 5-15 minutes per student.
- Adapt targeted practice: 10-20 minutes per student.
- Mark objective practice: 2-5 minutes per student.
- Prepare a parent update: 5-10 minutes per student.
- Target AI-draft review time: less than 2-3 minutes per student.

The relevant measurement is:

  net tutor time saved = manual baseline - assisted tutor review/edit time

Model processing time alone is not a valid productivity measure.

Selected first workflow
-----------------------

1. A student completes approved objective practice or a diagnostic.
2. Answers are marked deterministically and stored as immutable evidence.
3. The system derives a versioned mastery state using a deterministic policy.
4. A bounded diagnostic worker creates a weakness proposal that cites evidence,
   confidence, and policy version.
5. The tutor accepts, edits, rejects, or requests more evidence.
6. The system selects or drafts a short, differentiated practice set from
   approved sources.
7. The tutor approves the assessment draft.
8. Assignment occurs as a separate authorised action.
9. New answers become evidence for the next diagnostic cycle.

Must-have requirements
----------------------

- One class, one subject, one level, and three to five fractions sub-skills.
- A small tutor-approved question bank and curriculum corpus.
- Question-level answers and immutable evidence records.
- Deterministic grading for objective questions.
- Versioned mastery state derived by a deterministic policy.
- Weakness proposals containing evidence references, confidence, and policy
  version.
- Explicit insufficient-evidence and conflicting-evidence outcomes instead of
  invented conclusions.
- Different practice for students with different effective mastery states.
- Tutor actions to accept, edit, reject, or request more evidence.
- Approval and assignment as separate operations. Saving a draft must never
  create an assignment.
- Batch-friendly review and limited, actionable alerts.
- Server-side centre, role, tutor, student, and guardian access checks.
- An audit history for generated material, corrections, approvals,
  assignments, and delivery outcomes.
- A model-unavailable fallback using deterministic or pre-authored content.
- Synthetic data only for the public prototype and demonstration.
- A responsive student experience that works at phone-sized browser widths.

Human approval points
---------------------

1. An administrator or authorised tutor approves curriculum sources and
   question templates.
2. A tutor reviews uncertain, low-evidence, or conflicting diagnostic
   proposals.
3. A tutor approves an assessment draft before it can be assigned.
4. A tutor resolves generated-content errors and unsupported cases.
5. A tutor approves parent reports, and the system verifies guardian linkage
   and consent before delivery.

Nice-to-have and deferred requirements
--------------------------------------

- Multiple subjects, levels, and age groups.
- Attendance, billing, enrolment, and full LMS functionality.
- Autonomous grading of unrestricted free-text responses.
- Autonomous parent messaging.
- Voice, video, and advanced multimodal tutoring.
- Telegram integration when the responsive browser flow is sufficient.
- Broad centre analytics, model fine-tuning, production autoscaling, and
  elaborate deployment automation.

S0-01 decision record
---------------------

The tutor-capacity hypothesis is retained as an unvalidated product assumption,
not customer evidence. For the prototype, the team selects a one-class primary
Mathematics fractions workflow focused on evidence-backed diagnosis and
tutor-approved differentiated practice. Tutor approval is mandatory before
assessment assignment, and uncertain evidence is escalated. Parent reporting
remains a downstream workflow built on approved, versioned mastery history.
