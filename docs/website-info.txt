NUS-ISS SHOW ME YOUR AGENTS HACKATHON
Technical and Client Requirements (SME Category)

Source:
- https://www.iss.nus.edu.sg/show-me-your-agents
- https://services-iss.nus.edu.sg/Runtime/Runtime/Form/NUS-ISS+Show+Me+Your+Agents+Hackathon+Powered+by+AWS/

OBJECTIVE
Build a practical, secure, pilot-ready agentic AI solution for a real SME business problem. The intended outcomes are improved productivity, workflow automation, customer engagement, operational efficiency, and longer-term AI adoption/business transformation.

SME/CLIENT CONTEXT
- The solution should address a real business need, preferably from the participating SME's own operations.
- SMEs may submit business problem statements for consideration.
- SME problem statements may be curated and shared during the hackathon briefing; confirm the exact challenge-selection process at kickoff.
- The prototype should be useful beyond the hackathon and feasible to adopt, adapt, or pilot.

RELEVANT PROBLEM AREAS
- Administrative automation and repetitive workflow handling.
- Customer service, enquiry triage, and response drafting.
- Sales, marketing, lead qualification, and lead management.
- Supply chain, inventory, exception handling, and operations.
- Internal knowledge management and information retrieval.
- AI assistants that help SMEs operate more efficiently.

SOLUTION QUALITY CRITERIA
The official page expects solutions to be:
- Practical and relevant to SME needs.
- Feasible to deploy or pilot.
- Secure and responsible by design.
- Based on a sound technical architecture.
- An effective use of agentic AI.
- Able to demonstrate measurable business impact.

No detailed judging rubric has been published yet. Use the criteria above as the working technical/client acceptance criteria.

REFERENCE AGENT PATTERN
Business request
  -> Intake, classify, and extract information
  -> Retrieve relevant company knowledge/context
  -> Plan the next steps
  -> Use approved tools or business systems
  -> Validate the result
  -> Require human approval for sensitive or high-impact actions
  -> Execute the approved action
  -> Log the action, outcome, and measurable impact

The prototype should make the agent behaviour visible: multi-step planning, context/knowledge use, tool calls, decision points, exception handling, escalation, and an audit trail. Do not present only a generic chat interface without a meaningful business workflow behind it.

PROTOTYPE FLOW TEMPLATES
1. Administrative automation
   Email/document intake -> field extraction -> validation -> missing/invalid data follow-up -> human approval -> spreadsheet/ERP update -> notification -> audit log.

2. Customer service
   Customer enquiry -> intent classification -> retrieve approved policy/product information -> draft response with supporting sources -> confidence check -> human escalation or approval -> response/ticket logging.

3. Sales and marketing
   Lead intake -> information extraction/enrichment -> qualification -> recommended next action -> human approval -> CRM/task update -> outcome tracking.

4. Supply chain and operations
   Order/inventory/operations input -> detect exception -> retrieve relevant rules -> recommend action -> approval gate -> update operational system -> record result.

5. Knowledge management
   Upload or connect approved documents -> index/tag content -> retrieve relevant passages -> answer with sources -> escalate low-confidence answers -> feedback and usage log.

6. AI assistant
   User task request -> understand intent -> plan a multi-step task -> call authorised tools -> validate outputs -> ask for approval where required -> complete task -> provide a traceable summary.

For the demo, show three paths: a normal successful workflow, an incomplete/ambiguous input, and a safety or human-approval escalation.

MEASURABLE IMPACT
Define a baseline and compare it with the agent-assisted workflow. Useful measures include:
- Processing time and turnaround time.
- Number of manual steps or staff hours saved.
- Accuracy, completeness, and error rate.
- Response time and first-contact resolution for service workflows.
- Automation rate, escalation rate, and human override rate.
- Lead conversion, fulfilment, or operational exception outcomes where applicable.
- Estimated operating cost or AI-token cost.

TECHNICAL SUBMISSION EXPECTATIONS
Deliverables may include:
- Source code.
- Technical and user documentation.
- Working demonstration and presentation.
- Technical architecture diagram.
- Other materials requested by the organisers.

All submitted deliverables must be made publicly available under an organiser-approved open-source licence, using a public repository designated or approved by the organisers.

PREPARATION AND ORIGINALITY
- The terms state that submissions must be original work created during the hackathon unless otherwise permitted.
- Before the hackathon, prepare flows, mockups, architecture, synthetic fixtures, evaluation cases, and measurement plans.
- Do not assume that pre-built production code or a completed prototype can be submitted; confirm reuse rules with the organisers.
- Third-party libraries, datasets, models, and open-source components must have compatible licences and attribution.
- Generative AI tools are allowed, but material use may need to be disclosed if requested.

SECURITY, PRIVACY, AND RESPONSIBLE DESIGN
- Do not expose confidential, proprietary, customer, personal, financial, or commercially sensitive information.
- Use synthetic, anonymised, aggregated, or redacted data in prototypes, repositories, demos, and presentations.
- Keep SME confidential information private and use it only for the hackathon solution unless written permission is provided.
- Use approval gates for external, financial, customer-facing, or otherwise high-impact actions.
- Include validation, low-confidence escalation, traceable logs, and clear failure behaviour.
- Submissions must not contain malicious code, malware, harmful content, unlawful functionality, or unauthorised third-party material.
- Teams are responsible for safe and efficient AI-token usage; excessive or abusive consumption may result in disqualification.

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AND PUBLIC USE

- NUS-ISS/AWS receive a worldwide, perpetual, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence to use submitted materials for hackathon, education, research, publicity, and showcase purposes.
- The participating SME receives a non-exclusive licence to evaluate, test, and pilot the deliverables.
- Commercial deployment, production implementation, exclusive use, or further commercial development requires a separate written agreement.
- Ideas and solutions from the hackathon are intended as public resources and may be used, modified, and distributed under the applicable open-source licence.
